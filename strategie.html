The key insight

Stabiliser decides how much chlorine you need.

CYA protects chlorine from the sun but also slows it down. The more CYA, the higher free chlorine has to be. At CYA ≈ 100 the 1–3 ppm on the bottle does almost nothing.

≥ 8 ppmminimum FC at CYA 100
11–13 ppmtarget FC at CYA 100
39 ppmshock level (SLAM) at CYA 100

CYA does not break down by itself in a useful way. It only goes down by dilution. Both chlorine products on site (tablets, granules) add more of it.

Source: Trouble Free Pool, CYA/chlorine chart and SLAM guide (troublefreepool.com). Strip CYA is ±30 ppm, a turbidity drop test is ±10 ppm.

Water instead of buying water

Getting CYA down cheaply

Free

  • Stop adding CYA: no more tablets/dichlor as main chlorine
  • Lower the level by 10–15 cm in autumn and let winter rain refill it (≈ −10 % CYA)
  • Backwash water also dilutes a little

Costs money

  • Tanker water: 15–25 €/m³ (Spanish guide), a 30 % change ≈ 9.5 m³ ≈ 150–250 € OPEN
  • CYA reducer (enzymes): ≈ 105 €, unreliable, only above 18 °C, needs near-zero chlorine. Not in winter

Evaporation ≈ 17–19 m³/year, rain into the pool ≈ 9–12 m³/year (own estimate). Lowering the level: structural safety OPEN.

Equipment

Worth buying

Must have (≈ 150–200 €)

  • Telescopic pole + brush (steel brush is fine on bare concrete)
  • Leaf net
  • Manual vacuum head + hose via skimmer
  • Drop test kit with CYA turbidity test

Nice to have

  • Battery pool robot (≈ 180–500 €): optional. Helpers are available, so brushing by hand (also from inside the pool) does the same job
  • Floating chlorine dispenser, so tablets leave the skimmer
  • Leaf net / cover against olive leaves

Labour is not the bottleneck: Arne, Dennis or volunteers can get in and brush everything off. Do it before shocking, not at 39 ppm chlorine. Rough concrete holds algae better, so brushing is part of every rescue. Hardness should stay at 200–400 ppm for concrete.

Large surface only helps a biofilter, and bacteria die in chlorinated water. As a mechanical pre-filter it runs at ≈ 5 m/h against ≈ 41 m/h in the sand filter, so it adds little. It needs height or a second pump and grows algae when light gets in. Better value: leaf cover, skimmer sock, robot. Possible side use: tank for backwash water.

On-site checklist

  • 1Switch to liquid chlorine: buy a 25 L canister of hipoclorito sódico, a 2 L measuring jug and gloves for Gabi. Strip test, then pour 2–3 L (≈ +8–13 ppm). No new tablets or granules; the ones in the skimmer may dissolve.
  • 2Overnight test: strip in the evening and next morning, photos of both.
  • 3Filter: note pressure with pump on, look into the pump pre-filter basket.
  • 4Water temperature if a thermometer is around.
  • 5Labels: photo of the tablet bucket back (composition).
  • 6Shopping, if convenient: drop test kit with CYA, pole + brush + leaf net.
